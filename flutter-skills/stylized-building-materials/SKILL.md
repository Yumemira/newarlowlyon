# Stylized building materials

For geometry sketches use a few broad flat color areas and stop here; $building-sketch-guide handles that stage. Keep material families in a short art brief for later illustration. Do not model surface detail or read finish studies until material/art work is requested.

Give each material a recognizable surface and construction treatment while keeping the project's drawing language and lighting. This skill designs material expression; it does not replace architectural design, camera calibration or the image tool.

## Diagnose before choosing a palette

Inspect actual accepted art, relevant project direction and nearby buildings. Separate hue, light response, edge treatment, construction scale, wear and ornament. If every surface has the same rounded bevel, white rim and dense speckle, more material names may still produce one plastic-looking treatment.

Read [surface-language.md](references/surface-language.md) for material-specific cues and [richness-and-scale.md](references/richness-and-scale.md) for hierarchy and target-size checks. Do not freeze a numerical PBR preset as the definition of painted stone or metal. When rendering a 3D base, use shader controls as tools, inspect local API/version support and verify the image. When painting, express these cues through coherent values, shapes and edges.

## Write a material plan

For each important surface name its architectural location, substrate, finish, construction unit size, highlight character, edge treatment and an observable cue at the intended display size. A cue may combine color and form; “same surface but blue” is insufficient when the task calls for distinct materials. Keep substrate/finish separate: a painted or oxidized metal need not resemble bare polished metal everywhere.

Choose dominant masses, quieter supporting surfaces and focal details deliberately. Their proportions depend on the design; no universal 60/30/10 palette, fixed material count or compulsory glow. Richness may come from joinery, fitted stonework, carved depth, consistent finish or a single prominent assembly. Commonplace buildings can still be carefully designed.

Attach decorative materials to real geometry. Crystal glazing, a solid crystal mass and a luminous core are different choices. Record thickness/support and whether transparency, reflection or emission is intended. Avoid using glow to conceal an unsupported object or a broken roof joint. Mark speculative magical properties as visual proposals, not gameplay facts.

## Evaluate in the game view

Inspect the same design and light with different treatments, then compare neighboring building types. Read [sources-and-limits.md](references/sources-and-limits.md) for researched principles and their transfer limits. In a study, vary surface treatment without also changing camera/exposure/shape so the comparison has a clear cause.

Use declared gameplay sizes, or label exploratory sizes as probes. Compare full color, a value study and simplified material areas when useful. Grayscale does not prove or disprove a metal identity; silhouette cannot identify surface finish. Evaluate whether an intended cue survives and whether windows, entrances, people and nearby buildings retain priority. Check crystal/glass against at least light and dark grounds before final cutout acceptance.

Correct the failing cue: broaden a facet highlight, simplify vein patterns, reduce repetitive edge accents, enlarge a meaningful joint or move a rich focal material to a readable area. Do not compensate for a weak silhouette by increasing saturation. Read the imagegen skill when generating/editing raster output and recheck its result against the structural guide.

Deliver a material plan or requested artwork, target-size evidence and what remains unverified. A written recipe, shader setup or beautiful close-up alone does not establish successful game-scale material readability.
