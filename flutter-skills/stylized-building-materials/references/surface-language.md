# Surface language by construction

Conclusion: use material-specific relationships among light, edges and construction, rather than a uniform shiny outline on every surface. These are stylization options, not exact physical measurements.

| Surface | Large readable cue | Supporting detail | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| rough wood | grain follows member direction; broad matte value change | end grain and purposeful joins | identical noise on beam, cut end and plaster |
| finished wood | quieter grain, shaped panels and fitted edges | selected carved recesses or polished accents | uniformly glossy yellow bars |
| rough masonry | block mass, mortar/joins, uneven breaks | restrained mineral variation | each tiny stone outlined equally brightly |
| cut/polished stone | controlled planar edges, fitted courses, broad reflection where intended | veining or inlay follows surfaces | glass-like transparency or metal-like rims everywhere |
| bare metal | coherent strong value/reflection bands responding to form | formed seams, fasteners, selective patina | yellow paint called gold, or identical silver speckle everywhere |
| glass | surface reflection with a readable frame and intended view/tint | limited pane divisions, thickness cues | invisible walls or a white opaque slab |
| crystal | organized facets and selective contrasting planes | supported base, chosen internal/edge accents | every face emits equally and loses the volume |

Metal finish changes the response: cast, hammered, brushed, polished, painted and oxidized regions need not share one highlight. Polished stone can also reflect; distinguish it through architecture, pattern, color and response together rather than one threshold.

Wear should follow a reason: exposed edges, foot traffic, runoff, soot near a hot/exhaust assembly, handling at doors. This is a visual story proposal, not a claim about the building's simulated use. Avoid universal random chips or identical dirt masks; retain intentional clean areas.

Keep highlight direction and form readable across the building. Baked painted lighting must follow the project's lighting contract. Do not paint camera-facing white stripes independently on every new orientation and call the set consistent.
