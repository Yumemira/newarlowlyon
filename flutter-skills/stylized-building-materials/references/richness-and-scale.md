# Richness, focal hierarchy and scale

Conclusion: craftsmanship and a clear material hierarchy communicate wealth more reliably than indiscriminate detail. This is an art-direction hypothesis to check on the actual design.

Distinguish four controls: material choice, fabrication/precision, decorative treatment and maintenance. They need not increase together. An expensive workshop may be sooty around the work area; a modest shrine may be carefully maintained. Do not infer a game's wealth tier or racial rule from these visual possibilities.

Organize large quiet material fields around meaningful junctions and focal assemblies. Let metalwork make a gateway, canopy, exhaust or frame legible. Crystal can form a central structure rather than a scatter of tiny glowing ornaments. Carved wood can change depth and silhouette near an entrance without covering every beam in motifs.

At intended gameplay size, check the feature's projected size. If source texture features become fractional pixels after reduction, do not rely on their exact pattern. For example, 6 source pixels at a 0.2 display factor span 1.2 display pixels: they may alias or blend. This arithmetic is not a universal minimum detail rule. Use the actual renderer/filter and inspect the output.

Use 64/128/256-pixel exploratory probes only when game scale is unknown; label them as studies. Choose important cues large enough to survive the intended view. An enlarged review sheet can hide their disappearance, so include exact-size output.

Separate failures:
- material ambiguity: revise light/edge/construction cues;
- visual noise: simplify frequency and contrast distribution;
- loss of function: revise composition/openings, not only finish;
- shape/camera drift: return to construction/camera tools;
- edge contamination: review actual alpha compositing/export.

Grayscale helps inspect values but cannot alone classify a material; silhouette checks massing, not finish. Transparent materials must be checked on more than one ground. A grayscale or blur diagnostic should never overwrite production artwork.
