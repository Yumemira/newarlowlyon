# Materials as architectural choices

Conclusion: material differences should affect construction and visible finish, not only hue. The following are optional fantasy design tools, not historical or gameplay requirements.

Wood: rough structural members, carefully fitted boards, carved brackets, layered shingles, curved laminated-looking ribs, polished dark panels or restrained colored inlay. Show end grain/joinery where readable; changing timber species should not be the only variation between unrelated functions.

Stone: rubble base, larger dressed blocks, pale cut-stone arcades, dark polished slabs, colored bands, carved portals and mosaic/inlay. Let span, wall thickness and opening shapes suit the intended construction. Surface chips on every part are not the only way to add detail.

Metal: dark iron trusses and straps, copper/brass equipment, bronze reliefs, silver-toned trim, decorative roof sheets or a deliberately precious focal element. Distinguish metals through controlled highlights, value/color and edge treatment consistent with the painted scene. More gloss does not automatically look more expensive. Do not apply physically incompatible mirror reflections from different views.

Crystal: faceted inserts, a protected central lens, a rib-supported canopy, an architectural cluster or a ceremonial focal mass. Choose whether it is structural, glazed or decorative and model its attachments. Transparency or glow is optional. Tiny luminous gems scattered everywhere weaken distinctiveness and can obscure the real roof/wall boundaries.

Other finishes: ceramic/glazed surfaces, plaster, textile awnings, colored glass and enamel can enrich the palette without requiring a new architectural culture. Use material combinations that support the building's function and composition.

Describe each chosen material by role, location, treatment and game-scale read: “bronze exhaust hood over an open forge bay”, not merely “luxurious bronze”. Give dominant surfaces room to read, with subordinate trim and one or several deliberate focal areas. The appropriate hierarchy varies by building; no fixed material-count quota.

Richness is not mandatory everywhere. When the task calls for a humble building, give it intentional proportions and craftsmanship rather than making it an inferior copy of the same manor. When wealth is requested, change the quality and organization of the architecture along with finishes.

For light response, painted edge language, surface/finish distinctions and target-size critique, use stylized-building-materials when available. This card concerns architectural material placement, not universal shader values.
