# A reviewable brief and revision loop

Conclusion: a brief should make decisions inspectable and a critique should prescribe a specific change. Avoid self-awarded scores that have no evidence.

For each requested design record:
- purpose and project facts versus visual assumptions;
- shared camera/style references and their roles;
- activity/adjacency idea, footprint, dominant mass and roof section;
- material roles, focal assembly and intended craft/wealth treatment;
- nearest existing design and the structural relationship that distinguishes the proposal;
- expected view/zoom, preserved local identity and unresolved constraints.

When exploration is requested, offer genuinely different spatial concepts rather than automatically demanding three variations for every task. A useful counterexample is the existing repeated house: describe the visible inheritance and why the proposed change fixes it. Do not write “unique” as the only distinction.

Critique as observation → possible cause → concrete revision → check. Example: “At the reduced view the crystal is a white blob; equal emission on all facets may erase form; reduce emission, group facet values and keep a supported dark base; recheck both backgrounds at the same size.” The cause is a hypothesis until compared.

Review new design against its nearest neighbor, material expression at the intended size, geometry/contact and camera/scene compatibility. Record pass, revise or unknown for each axis with evidence. Keep possible role ambiguity separate from clearly incorrect topology. If the display size is unknown, an exploratory size check cannot establish final acceptance.

After a change, recheck the affected axis plus any it could disturb. A new roof silhouette needs contact/framing checks; a highlight change needs value/readability review; neither demands unrelated engine testing. Stop when requested acceptance is met or identify the concrete unresolved defect. A fresh prompt with more adjectives is not a diagnosis.

Do not pretend to run an independent or blind evaluation when the author performed the review. A concept table checks design reasoning, not final image quality. Keep user approval, visual inspection and tool validation as different evidence.
